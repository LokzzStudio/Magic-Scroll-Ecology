Rulebook

The whole game, sorted two ways. Rules of the game are the facts of the world: what Power and Intelligence do, what a day's work makes, how people are born, grow up and die. A player can learn them and plan around them. How the simulation runs is everything else the villagers need to act on their own: the order they take jobs in, how they share the work, when they walk home, how the numbers are rounded. Some of those are the player's to change; the rest are choices made for them, for now. Numbers are the defaults; each page's Implementation details has the exact rules.

Rules of the game explicit

What the world is like. These would hold whoever ran the village.

Time and place Map

  • A year is 360 days: spring, summer, autumn and winter, 90 days each. A run lasts 50 years.
  • The map is 16 × 16 tiles: houses, a farm, a pasture, a forest, a mine, the well (井) and the river (川), where farmers fetch water (from the Watering page on). Walking takes 1 minute a tile.
  • A standard day's work is 100 energy.

Life Humans

  • People live about 3,600 days (±10%). Children until day 360, adults until 3,240, then elders.
  • An adult man and woman pair for life; only couples of two adults have children.
  • A child is born 300 days after it's conceived, if the mother lives (the father needn't: the child still takes after him). A woman expecting doesn't conceive again, and conceives only while she could give birth before she's an elder. So a new couple has no child in their first 300 days. you: Humans page
  • Each person's chance of conceiving is 0.01 a day × happiness ÷ 50; a couple's is the average of the two.
  • Births lean toward the scarcer sex: a boy with chance (females + X) ÷ (males + females + 2X), birth balance X = 1.
  • A village of fewer than 10 people may draw an outsider: each day one joins with chance 1 ÷ (people + 1)² ÷ 10, aged 360 to 1,440 days, a man or woman by the same rule as births, with Power and Intelligence each drawn evenly from 80 to 100. An empty village is rescued the same way (1 in 10 a day).
  • A day's food need is a person's Power, children and elders too (before Power exists: 100 for an adult, 70 for a child, 80 for an elder).
  • Hunger shortens life by 1 day for each food missed, counted over a life and rounded down.
  • Being hungry during the day (past a meal, at home or out) shows, but costs nothing.

Power and Intelligence Attribute

  • Power is a day's energy: ⌊Power⌋ a day.
  • Intelligence is speed: Intelligence ÷ 10 energy an hour, so the clever get more done before the day ends.
  • Elders work at 80% of both. Scholars have 90% of their Power and 110% of their Intelligence.
  • Nobody's Power or Intelligence goes below 70; a child's below 40, so they come of age at 70 or more.

Growing up Inheritance · Education

  • A newborn's Power and Intelligence are each 0.7 × the average of their parents', give or take 15% (before there are couples, a random adult man and woman stand in).
  • Children turn extra milk into Power and lessons into Intelligence, one point at a time. The next point costs (max(70, A) − 70)² + 100: cheap up to 70, then dearer and dearer.
  • A child going hungry loses 1 Power for every 10 food missed, down to 40.
  • Children eat first, milk first; once the grown-ups have eaten their need too, the children drink all the milk left: extra milk.
  • At 360 days they come of age: +30 to both. After that, attributes don't change.
  • Scholars teach at the house: a child learns the slower of the two's speed × the time in class × teaching skill, shared among the class. Scholars do no other work.

Happiness Happiness

  • Happiness = 150 − Intelligence (at least 0), plus ⌊energy left unspent yesterday ÷ 10⌋, at most 100.
  • So the clever are less happy and have fewer children, and rest makes people happier.

Food Food

  • 1 energy milking an adult cow gives 1 milk. One milker to a cow a day, but no limit on what one cow gives. Calves (under 60 days) give none.
  • 4 fields, one person to each a day. Tending (days 1–180): 1 energy grows 1 rice, at most 50,000 a field a year. Harvest (days 181–270): 1 energy brings in 25 rice; whatever's left after day 270 is lost.
  • Milk keeps 1 day, rice 360. The village starts with 50,000 food that never spoils.

Herds Herd

  • Cows and sheep live about 360 days (±15%). A herd grows by r·N·(N/A − 1)·(1 − N/K) a day (r 0.02, A 2, K 10): it shrinks when very small or crowded, grows in between.
  • Sheep grow back 30 wool a day, up to 300.

Resources Resources

  • 1 energy gives 1 wool (shearing a sheep), 1 timber (forest) or 1 stone (mine). The forest and mine never run out.
  • Only so many can work a place in a day: a shearer to each sheep with wool, at most 2 quarriers at the mine and 3 woodcutters in the forest.

Skills Resources

  • Nine skills: milking, farming, woodcutting, weaving, forging, shearing, quarrying, teaching, poetry. Every energy spent at a job is 1 exp in its skill.
  • Levels 1–4 take 100, 1,000, 10,000 and 100,000 more exp. Each level makes the work more effective: +10%, +20%, +30%, +40%.
  • Effectiveness is more out of the same work (milk, rice, wool, timber, stone, knowledge), or more speed where the amount is fixed (the harvest, crafts).
  • Skills aren't inherited: everyone starts at level 0.

Crafts and tools Craft

  • Women weave 衣 clothes (200 wool + 100 timber) and 笠 hats (100 wool + 200 timber); men forge 刀 blades (200 stone + 100 timber) and 叉 hayforks (100 stone + 200 timber). Each takes 100 energy.
  • One loom and one forge, one crafter at each a day. Weaving and forging each use a blade; without one they still can, but it counts 0 toward the craft's tier.
  • A craft's tier comes from its materials' tier, its crafter's skill and their blade's tier (0 without one), then a lucky roll.
  • A hayfork adds to tending effectiveness, and a blade to the harvest (收割), to collecting and to crafting: +20% to +100%, by tier. Clothes and hats are worn by everyone (on the Clothing page, only against the cold and the rain).
  • Tools wear out with use: each after 2,000 energy (clothes and hats: all their wearer spends; blades: the harvest, collecting and crafting; hayforks: tending).
  • The village starts with no blades, hayforks, clothes or hats in store (any number of R ones can be set). you: Craft page

Tiers Resources · Craft

  • N, R, SR, SSR, and for crafts only UR. Animals are at most SR.
  • Wool, timber and stone: a lot's tier is the average of what went in (the worker's skill, the sheep or the place, and from the Craft page on the worker's blade, none counting as N), give or take up to half a tier.
  • A craft lands halfway between its score (the average of its materials' tier, its maker's skill and their blade's tier) and a lucky roll from 1.01 up to one more than the score: round((score + roll) ÷ 2). The better the inputs, the harder it pulls down, so high tiers are rare and hard-won.
  • With everything SSR, a craft is SSR half the time, and UR only 1 in 400.

Weather Weather

  • Clouds blow across the map; the more clouds, the likelier rain, and it snows below 0 °C.
  • The sky goes in spells, clear and cloudy by turns. The calmer a season's wind, the longer they last: a clear and a cloudy spell together take as long as 16 crossings of the sky.
  • Each season has its own clouds, wind, rain and temperature. Cloudy and rainy days are cooler, and the temperature swings through the day, coldest at 06:00 and warmest at 15:00.
  • Rain raises the water level and dry hours drain it: at 0 a drought, at 10 a flood.
  • The mountain above the river holds water the same way, but doesn't drain below −5 °C, when the river is frozen. The river runs as long as the mountain has water.
  • From the Clothing page on, the village feels the weather's temperature and its rain.

Watering Watering

  • The river (川) runs while the mountain has water: it's dry only in the deepest droughts, about one year in 35. The well holds 100 water. It refills from the ground (half the land's water level an hour, up to 50) and from the rain (its rainfall an hour, up to 100).
  • Each field has its own water level, 0–10. Tending uses 0.1 level per energy; a field nobody tends goes back to the land's level within the day.
  • A field tended this year grows rice by itself, by its water level: +30 a day at levels 3–4, +20 at 2 and 5, +10 at 1 and 6, nothing at 7, and it loses rice when too dry or too wet (−100 at 0 or 10).

The player Strategy

  • The player may kill any villager, at any time.
  • They may appoint any grown-ups as scholars, as many as they like, and dismiss them.
  • Once a month they may give each villager their own order of jobs, which may include 發呆: doing nothing for the rest of the day.
  • For each couple they may say whether they may have a child.
  • They may fill the well whenever they like, and give any number of 刀 叉 衣 笠, up to SR.

The rain Clothing

  • Rain comes in levels from 0 to 10. Out of the house it slows the work: at level R, by (R ÷ 10)² (level 3: 9%, level 4: 16%).
  • A 笠 keeps off 2 levels a tier (N −2 to UR −10).
  • Nobody goes home for the rain, however heavy: at 10 (as felt) the work does nothing. From 5 on they go out in a 笠.
  • Rain burns a fire down faster: at level R it lasts (R ÷ 10)² less long. At 10 none can be lit.

The cold Clothing

  • The house is warm. Out of it, below 0 °C (as it feels), people fall into hypothermia. It costs no days of life, only work.
  • Below 0 °C their work out of the house slows by (felt ÷ 10)², as in the rain. At −5 °C or colder they go home.
  • A 衣 feels warmer by 5 °C a tier (N +5 to UR +25).
  • A fire, lit with 1 timber and 1 energy, burns 2 hours and warms everyone within 4 tiles by 5 °C a tier of its timber (N +5 to SSR +20), on top of any 衣.

How the simulation runs implicit

What the villagers need so they can act without being told: habits, timing and bookkeeping. Change these and the world stays the same, but the village behaves differently.

you: Priority the player sets it, with the control namedfixed chosen for the villagers, for now

Choosing work

  • Jobs are taken in an order: fields, milking, crafts, collecting, education, poetry. you: Priority
  • Who may do which job: by default children milk and go to class, men and women farm and cut wood, women shear and weave, men quarry and forge. you: Jobs
  • Those who can only milk get the cows first; anyone who could also farm takes a cow only if one is spare. fixed
  • Children do only the food work the grown-ups can't cover, and spend the rest of the day in class. fixed
  • Collecting: each grown-up starts with their own (women wool, men stone) or timber, whichever the village has less of, then the other, then the other sex's. fixed
  • A station makes next whichever of its items has been started fewest times; its crafter keeps it for the day. fixed
  • Tools: everyone takes the best clothes and hat left; the best hayfork or blade is taken on the way to the job and put back if they don't get it. fixed

Eating Food

  • Children eat first: their full need, milk first, while there's food. fixed
  • Then the grown-ups and elders: their need, or short of it, each the same share of their need, all that's left. Nobody rations. fixed
  • The food that spoils soonest is eaten first, the starting food last. fixed

Scholars and class Education

  • While there are children to teach, the village appoints one scholar: the adult with the highest Intelligence, but only if it can spare them (at least 4 grown-ups must still be able to tend). fixed
  • More scholars, one per child at most, while the milkers left still cover the village's need. you: in the game
  • With more than one scholar, the children are split evenly and at random among them. fixed
  • Teaching or poetry first follows the Priority order. you: Priority

The shape of a day Map

  • People wake between 4 and 8 o'clock, spread at random. fixed
  • Everyone is out of the house for 12 hours. you: Map page
  • Breakfast at the house (24 minutes); from 5½ hours, home for lunch once the work under way is done. you: Map page
  • A meal keeps someone full for 6 hours on a full share, less on less. fixed
  • Idle, out of energy and hungry, they go in early rather than wait outside. fixed

Working, step by step

  • Work goes 10 energy at a time, and a unit starts only if they could finish it and be home by the end of the day. fixed
  • Whatever they collect (milk, the harvest, wool, timber, stone) is carried home after every 10 energy of it, and at the end of the job. you: Map page
  • Taking a shared job reserves what they could still do there, so two people don't plan the same work. fixed
  • With nothing to do they wait at the house. When someone gives up a job with work left, everyone waiting chooses again. fixed

Pairing Happiness

  • Each night, single adult men and women pair up at random, as many as the smaller group. fixed
  • A couple ends only when a partner dies. fixed

Bookkeeping

  • Each day goes in order: the player acts (Strategy page), food ages and spoils, everyone works, the day's take goes into store, everyone eats, then hunger, deaths, pairing, births and newcomers.
  • Energy, food made and attributes round down; fractions of food from work carry over to the next day.
  • Wool, timber and stone are stored in lots of 100, each lot one tier. Crafts use the best tiers first.
  • Skill levels are counted at the end of the day, for the next.

Fetching water Watering

  • A farmer who finds their field at level 3 or lower fetches 1 water (+1 level): from the well if it has water and is nearer, otherwise from the river if it's running. fixed
  • They look when they get to the field and after each bit of work. fixed

Facing the rain Clothing

  • Nobody goes home for the rain: in any rain they work on, slower. fixed
  • Leaving the house in heavy rain (5 or more), they put on the 笠 of the lowest tier that brings it under 5, saving the better ones. fixed
  • With no 笠 good enough, they take the best there is, and go anyway. fixed
  • The work's cut goes by the rain as each bit of work starts. fixed

Facing the cold Clothing

  • Leaving the house on a freezing day, they put on the 衣 of the lowest tier that's warm enough, saving the warmer ones. fixed
  • If no 衣 is enough, they wear the warmest there is and take a timber of the lowest tier that makes up the rest, to light where they work. fixed
  • If it would still feel −5 °C or colder, they stay in and look again on the hour; warmer than that, they go and put up with it. fixed
  • At −5 °C or colder at work, they finish what's in hand and walk home to warm up. fixed
  • Indoors from the cold they still eat breakfast and lunch. fixed

Strategies Strategy

  • Most people: holds the village under 20 people, about what the cows and fields feed; one scholar once there are 6 adults; the well full and SR crafts for all. you: Strategy
  • Brightest person: as Most people, and the dullest adult goes now and then. you: Strategy
  • Sustainable: builds the village up for 10 years, stocks it with SR crafts, then lets go. you: Strategy
  • A UR craft: the two brightest adults on the loom and forge first while food is plentiful, and no more 刀 given once the village has made an SSR one. you: Strategy

Running the simulation

  • Each page plays one village (Roll new runs for other luck), on separate dice for cows, sheep, people, births, waking, classes, pairing, tiers and the weather, so every setting faces the same luck.
  • A village survives if anyone is alive after 50 years. A run stops past 2,000 people, counted as surviving.
  • Micro mode is the game's rules, person by person. Macro mode is a quick copy for the whole village: it averages over wake-up times for class hours, for example, and in the weather it works out one person's whole day at a time, everyone waking at an even spread, so a fire lit by someone worked out later can't warm them, and someone kept in waits for the same job rather than choosing another. It runs by default.
