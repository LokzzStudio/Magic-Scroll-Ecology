Weather

Clouds blow across the village's sky. The weather goes in spells: in a clear spell no clouds come; in a cloudy spell they come in at the west edge at random, drift east with the wind and leave at the east edge. The sky holds at most 10 clouds; a cloud due while it's full doesn't come. Four settings shape the clouds: how often they come in a cloudy spell, how fast the wind carries them across on average, how much it varies, and how much of the time is cloudy. The weather moves with the wind: a clear and a cloudy spell together last as long as the wind takes to cross the sky 16 times, so in a calm season the spells are long (the sky you see is likely still the same half a day later) and in a windy one the sky changes often. Rain comes from the clouds: the more there are, the likelier it rains, and the heavier. Each season has its own settings, temperature too: a day is warmer the sunnier it is, and rain falls as snow below 0 °C. A water level from 0 to 10 rises with rain and falls in dry hours: at 0 it's a drought, at 10 a flood. The mountain above the river (川) keeps a level of its own the same way, except that it doesn't drain while it's below −5 °C (the river frozen): the river runs as long as it holds any water.
